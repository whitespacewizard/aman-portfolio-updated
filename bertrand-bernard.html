Opportunity

Three connected pricing and product-framing gaps were holding back trial quality and paid conversion.

01 Free tools set the category baseline. Buyers already had access to free crop-health maps and basic advisory. The platform’s stronger decision layer across disease risk, irrigation, harvesting and compliance was not visible enough during the buying journey.

02 Feature gating hid the product’s real value. Sixty-four percent of trials started on the entry tier, but that tier excluded disease alerts, irrigation planning, yield forecasting and scouting tools. Buyers were evaluating a limited version of the product instead of experiencing the capabilities that differentiated it.

03 Pricing created avoidable buying friction. The pricing page had a 52% exit rate and organised value around feature access rather than the amount of land being monitored. The model needed to align with a unit customers already understood and used for budgeting.

Solution

The pricing model was rebuilt around land under monitoring, while the product experience and experiment setup were aligned to the same logic.

01 Full-product access with acre-based metering

Instead of restricting capabilities by plan, every plan received the full product. Pricing moved to acres under monitoring, a unit customers already understood and used for budgeting.

01 Feature gates were removed

Disease alerts, irrigation planning, yield forecasting and scouting tools were no longer held back by the entry tier.

02 Land became the pricing unit

Plans were metered by acres under monitoring rather than by access to different product capabilities.

03 The pricing page was reframed

The buying experience presented the offer around monitored acreage, making the commercial model easier to understand and budget for.

04 Product selection flows were updated

The signup flow and in-app plan selector were changed to reflect the acre-based model consistently.

05 Entitlements and testing were aligned

Entitlement logic was updated and the new model was tested through a six-week, 50/50 A/B test across 18,412 visitors.

A grower with 800 acres and a co-operative with 90,000 acres need the same tools. They simply have different amounts of land to monitor. Feature gating restricted comprehension; acre-based pricing restricted consumption, which is easier for buyers to understand and scale.

Opportunity

Three connected outbound gaps had to be solved before outreach could scale safely and remain relevant.

01 Targeting needed exhibition-level precision. The client needed to reach companies actively preparing for trade shows, not a broad list of businesses. The ICP had to connect the right company, decision-maker and upcoming exhibition so each message arrived in a commercially relevant context.

02 Prospect data had to support personalisation. Scaling with incomplete or unverified records would reduce relevance, increase failed deliveries and weaken campaign quality. The database needed cleanup, verification and segmentation before it could reliably support personalised variables and message variations.

03 Sending and follow-up needed a safe system. A single inbox or one-off email could not create repeatable opportunity flow. The campaign required warmed infrastructure, controlled daily volume and a multi-step sequence that could follow up consistently without sacrificing deliverability.

Solution

Targeting, prospect data, sending infrastructure and follow-up were built as one connected outbound system rather than as separate campaign tasks.

01 ICP to campaign execution

The campaign combined exhibition-level targeting, verified prospect data, warmed infrastructure and a multi-step sequence so outreach could scale while remaining relevant and deliverability-safe.

01 The ICP was tied to active exhibitions

Companies, decision-makers and upcoming trade shows were connected so each message reached a commercially relevant prospect.

02 Prospect data was prepared for personalisation

The lead database was sourced, cleaned, verified and segmented before it was used for personalised variables and message variations.

03 Sending capacity was distributed safely

44 warmed inboxes were capped at 25 emails per account per day, creating a controlled daily capacity of 1,100 emails.

04 A five-follow-up sequence was deployed

Campaigns used multi-step sequences, message variations, personalised variables and soft calls to action.

05 Replies guided ongoing iteration

Most replies appeared after the second or third touchpoint, helping focus sequence operations on consistent follow-up rather than one-off sends.

My contribution ICP to campaign execution

Owned ICP definition, prospect-data sourcing and cleanup, segmentation, campaign setup, sequence operations and initial scripting.

Results

The distributed infrastructure delivered 7,700 sends without relying on a single inbox or uncontrolled volume, producing measurable reply and opportunity flow over seven days.

Opportunity

Three connected account gaps were limiting conversion output and making paid acquisition far more expensive than it needed to be.

01 Conversion signals were too weak. Lead-form and conversion tracking were not giving Google Ads enough dependable signal quality. With limited measurement data, automated bidding had less reliable information to identify and optimise toward users more likely to complete a valuable action.

02 Weak tests were consuming budget. Underperforming campaigns and experiments were creating budget bleed, while spend was not concentrated strongly enough around higher-intent queries and campaigns. The account needed clearer pruning and allocation decisions before additional budget could work efficiently.

03 Reach was not translating into efficient acquisition. From January to June 2023, approximately $58.0K in spend and 388K impressions produced only 48 measured conversions at roughly $1.21K per conversion. The account had visibility, but not enough qualified conversion output for the investment.

Solution

The account was improved through five connected changes that strengthened measurement, removed inefficient activity and directed spend toward higher-intent opportunities.

01 Campaign structure and intent focus

Campaign cleanup, bidding learning and budget allocation were handled as one system, so spend moved away from weak tests and toward searches and markets with stronger lead potential.

01 Tracking and signal quality improved

Lead-form and conversion signals were strengthened so optimisation was based on clearer, more dependable measured actions.

02 Smart Bidding had more learning data

More conversion history gave Maximise Conversions a stronger base for identifying users likely to complete a valuable action.

03 Weak tests were pruned

Underperforming campaigns and experiments were reduced, paused or replaced to limit budget bleed.

04 Budget moved toward higher intent

Spend was redirected toward campaigns and queries more likely to produce trackable lead actions.

05 New-market expansion was tested

New Zealand and USA activity introduced additional audience pools beyond the earlier market setup.

Results

Conversion output increased sharply in the second half of 2023, while cost per conversion fell despite only a modest increase in media spend.
